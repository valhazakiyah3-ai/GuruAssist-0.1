# Assessment and alignment checks

## Alignment map

For each learning objective, identify:

1. Evidence: what learner performance or product would demonstrate achievement?
2. Activity: what learning experience prepares learners to produce that evidence?
3. Assessment: what task or observation collects the evidence?
4. Criteria: what counts as successful performance?

## Assessment design checks

- The task measures the stated objective, not an unrelated skill.
- Instructions are clear and accessible.
- Difficulty is appropriate for the learners and time available.
- Scoring criteria are transparent.
- Answer keys are checked against the supplied source material.
- Performance tasks have observable criteria.
- Formative feedback tells learners what to improve next.

## Avoid false precision

Do not assign arbitrary percentages, passing thresholds, or grade bands unless the teacher supplies the applicable policy. If a sample rubric uses illustrative levels, label them as adjustable examples.
